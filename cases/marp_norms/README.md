# Religion, well-being and cultural norms (MARP, RQ2)

Curated Borges case on real, unplanted data from the Many Analysts Religion Project (MARP), in
which 120 research teams analysed the same dataset. This case is the second research question:
does the relation between religiosity and well-being depend on perceived cultural norms of
religion? The companion case `marp_religiosity` is the first (RQ1).

## Data

- Dataset: "Many Analysts Religion Project: Data on Religiosity and Well-being", 10,535
  participants in 24 countries. Source: https://osf.io/qbdce/ (file
  https://osf.io/download/k9puq/, 2.9 MB). `data.parquet` keeps the columns used here
  (religiosity, cultural-norm and well-being items and means, age, gender, SES, education,
  scaled GDP, attention check, country) in compact dtypes; the free-text and demographic columns
  the case does not use are dropped.
- Team results: `MARP_data_main.csv` from the project "MARP Analysis" (https://osf.io/gkxqy/,
  https://osf.io/download/bw863/). It holds one row per team with the standardised effect and
  95% interval for RQ1 and RQ2: 110 teams reported one for RQ1 and 105 for RQ2. These are the
  reference lane of the case, exactly as the 29 teams are for `red_cards`.
- Team choices used to seed the plan: `variable_coding.csv` (https://osf.io/download/g9sfm/),
  the per-team coding of statistical model, outcome, predictors and covariates, plus the
  free-text reports in `MARP_data_main.csv`.
- License: CC-By Attribution 4.0 International, read from the OSF API (`/v2/nodes/qbdce/` and
  `/v2/nodes/gkxqy/`, `node_license`). Redistribution with attribution is permitted.
- Required attribution: Hoogeveen, S., Sarafoglou, A., Aczel, B., et al. (2022). A many-analysts
  approach to the relation between religiosity and well-being. Religion, Brain & Behavior.
  https://doi.org/10.1080/2153599X.2022.2070255

## The case

Decision rule: the standardised religiosity x perceived-cultural-norms interaction on well-being
is greater than 0 at p < 0.05. The option space (religiosity measure, well-being measure,
norms measure, exclusions, covariates, standardisation, model family) is seeded from the
choices the teams published; every option's `source` field says how many teams made it. The
scripted adversary proposes nothing: no adversary argument was invented. The scripted narrator
is generated by `build_fixtures.py` from the numbers of a scripted run, so it states only
computed values.

Well-being options are alternative measures of overall well-being, as in `marp_religiosity`.
Norms are measured either as each person's own perception or as the country mean of it.

## Result (scripted run, 15 universes)

- Baseline (random country intercept, individual norm perception): interaction beta = 0.045,
  95% CI 0.026 to 0.063, p < 0.001.
- A flip exists with a single change: pooled OLS with robust errors gives 0.018, 95% CI -0.000
  to 0.037, p = 0.056. It reproduced exactly in a fresh sandbox. The estimate stays positive; it
  is the p-value that crosses 0.05, so this is the nearest p-value crossing, not a sign reversal.
- Enumerating all 1,536 universes offline (`grid_summary.json`): 162 (10.5%) change the verdict;
  1 by one change, 9 by two, 36 by three, 65 by four, 42 by five and 9 by six. Estimates run
  from -0.002 to 0.049. The search found the single flip because it tries singles first, which
  biases it toward small flips. The separate re-run in `evidence/grid_vs_search.md` counts 161
  flipping universes (89.5% agree) instead of 162; the difference is one universe.
- The 105 teams: interaction beta -0.045 to 0.282, median 0.039; 68 of 105 (65%) have an
  interval above zero. Borges's universes agree with the baseline more often (89%) than the
  teams do (65%) and span a narrower range: the option space is the teams' common choices, not
  their tails.
- Of the 162 verdict-changing universes, 92 are multilevel fits and 58 of those carry the
  statsmodels warning that the Hessian is not positive definite (a variance on the boundary);
  the verified single flip is pooled OLS and does not.

Read together with `marp_religiosity` (768 of 768 universes agree): the same data and the same
search separate a conclusion that no analyst choice dents from one that depends on the model
family.

## Files

- `prepare.py` downloads the raw files into `data/raw/` (git-ignored) and writes the parquet
  for both MARP cases.
- `option_code.py` exposes the option code of the plan; the code lives in `../marp_shared/`.
- `build_fixtures.py` writes `fixtures/` and `case.json`, runs the case once with the scripted
  provider, and builds the narrator from that run. `--grid` also rewrites `grid_summary.json`.
- `recorded/events.jsonl` is `borges record-demo --case marp_norms --llm scripted`.
- `recorded-live/events.jsonl` is the live Nemotron recording of 5 October 2026 (see
  `evidence/live-runs.md`); the web app plays it by default.

The fixtures are hand-built, not model output: the usage they carry is zero and the model field reads `scripted-fixture`. Regenerate with
`uv run --no-sync python cases/marp_norms/prepare.py` then
`uv run --no-sync python cases/marp_norms/build_fixtures.py --grid`.
