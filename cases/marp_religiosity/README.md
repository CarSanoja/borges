# Religion and well-being (MARP, RQ1)

Curated Borges case on real, unplanted data from the Many Analysts Religion Project (MARP), in
which 120 research teams analysed the same dataset to answer: do religious people self-report
greater well-being?

## Data

- Dataset: "Many Analysts Religion Project: Data on Religiosity and Well-being", 10,535
  participants in 24 countries. Source: https://osf.io/qbdce/ (file
  https://osf.io/download/k9puq/, 2.9 MB). `data.parquet` keeps the columns used here
  (religiosity, cultural-norm and well-being items and means, age, gender, SES, education,
  scaled GDP, attention check, country) in compact dtypes; the free-text and demographic columns
  the case does not use are dropped.
- Team results: `MARP_data_main.csv` from the project "MARP Analysis" (https://osf.io/gkxqy/,
  https://osf.io/download/bw863/). It holds one row per team with the standardised effect and
  95% interval for RQ1 and RQ2: 110 teams reported one for RQ1 and 105 for RQ2. These are the
  reference lane of the case, exactly as the 29 teams are for `red_cards`.
- Team choices used to seed the plan: `variable_coding.csv` (https://osf.io/download/g9sfm/),
  the per-team coding of statistical model, outcome, predictors and covariates, plus the
  free-text reports in `MARP_data_main.csv`.
- License: CC-By Attribution 4.0 International, read from the OSF API (`/v2/nodes/qbdce/` and
  `/v2/nodes/gkxqy/`, `node_license`). Redistribution with attribution is permitted.
- Required attribution: Hoogeveen, S., Sarafoglou, A., Aczel, B., et al. (2022). A many-analysts
  approach to the relation between religiosity and well-being. Religion, Brain & Behavior.
  https://doi.org/10.1080/2153599X.2022.2070255

## The case

Decision rule: the standardised coefficient of religiosity on well-being is greater than 0 at
p < 0.05. The option space (religiosity measure, well-being measure, exclusions, covariates,
standardisation, model family) is seeded from the choices the teams published; every option's
`source` field says how many teams made it. The scripted adversary proposes nothing: no
adversary argument was invented. The scripted narrator is generated by `build_fixtures.py`
from the numbers of a scripted run, so it states only computed values.

Well-being options are alternative measures of overall well-being (the 18-item composite, 17
items without the sexual satisfaction item, the mean of the three subscale means, the two
general items). A single facet answers a narrower question and is not in the space; see the
sensitivity below.

## Result (scripted run, 40 universes)

- Baseline (random country intercept, all nine religiosity items, overall well-being, no
  covariates): beta = 0.140, 95% CI 0.119 to 0.160, p < 0.001.
- No flip found: the 40 universes run beta 0.064 to 0.150, every interval above zero.
- Enumerating all 768 universes offline (`grid_summary.json`): all 768 keep the verdict,
  beta 0.053 to 0.156.
- The 110 teams: beta 0.007 to 0.354, median 0.120, all 110 intervals above zero. Borges's
  range is narrower than the teams' (the option space has no unusual outcome or estimator) but
  agrees on sign and significance with every team.

## Sensitivity outside the option space

Single well-being facets, baseline model, standardised beta (p): psychological 0.192 (<0.001),
social 0.122 (<0.001), physical 0.042 (<0.001). The physical facet is the fragile one: with
age, gender, SES and education it falls to 0.017 (p = 0.087), and to -0.018 (p = 0.055) under
pooled OLS. So "religious people report greater overall well-being" is robust in this space;
"religious people report better physical health" is not. `tests/cases/test_marp.py` pins these
numbers.

Some multilevel fits (64 of 384 in the grid) sit on a variance boundary, where statsmodels warns
that the Hessian is not positive definite; they are kept, and all of them are positive.

## Files

- `prepare.py` downloads the raw files into `data/raw/` (git-ignored) and writes the parquet
  for both MARP cases.
- `option_code.py` exposes the option code of the plan; the code lives in `../marp_shared/`.
- `build_fixtures.py` writes `fixtures/` and `case.json`, runs the case once with the scripted
  provider, and builds the narrator from that run. `--grid` also rewrites `grid_summary.json`.
- `recorded/events.jsonl` is `borges record-demo --case marp_religiosity --llm scripted`.

The fixtures are hand-built, not model output, and no live Nemotron call was made for this case:
the usage they carry is zero and the model field reads `scripted-fixture`. Regenerate with
`uv run --no-sync python cases/marp_religiosity/prepare.py` then
`uv run --no-sync python cases/marp_religiosity/build_fixtures.py --grid`.
